Before the ages

The constants

A world without a beginning

No one remembers how the world began. Its geography shifts, its landmarks vanish, and no traveller has found its edge. A person could walk through the mist forever without returning to where they started.

Through it all, Loot and the sixteen Orders have persisted. Some believe Loot is woven into the world itself: never truly lost, always found again. Others believe it created the world.

From Loot emerged the Realms. Its power established societies that endured through the ages. Kingdoms rose and fell, and the Realms changed hands, but they remained anchors in a shifting world. An ancient civilisation raised the Hyperstructures in devotion to Loot and the Orders. For millennia, these colossal monuments stood as centres of power and influence.

Then Loot vanished overnight. No one knew why. The societies it had sustained fractured, and the world clung to what remained: the Realms and the Hyperstructures.

Age I

Blitz

Chaos & war

The struggle for what remained

Thousands of kingdoms rose and fell in the struggle for the Hyperstructures. No one understood how the ancient monuments worked, but whoever held them commanded the land. Their rulers sought power and answers. Perhaps the monuments held a clue to Loot’s disappearance, or a path to its return.

Then the Spires emerged from the land. Their peaks hung in the air, silent and unmoving. No one knew what they were, or what might awaken them.

One day, a Lord claimed a Hyperstructure, as countless others had before. Every Spire awoke. Portals opened beneath their floating peaks, offering passage through the ethereal layer to any other Spire a traveller had discovered. The Hyperstructures held these passages in balance. Lords crossed the land in an instant and turned this new power against their rivals.

As the Spires multiplied, the boundary between worlds wore thin. Reality warped. The air grew toxic, and a thick, dark mist spread across the land. Beasts emerged from the ethereal layer. Kingdoms fell, and the survivors retreated until everything lay in darkness.

Everything except the Realms.

Age II

Frontier

The dark age

The last lights of the world

The last lights of the world shone within the Realms. Beyond them, the mist swallowed the ruins of civilisation, erasing its history with every place it destroyed.

Lords sent soldiers and expeditions to reestablish the frontier. They fought the beasts, pushed back the darkness and gathered resources to strengthen their Realms. Some entered the Spires, venturing into the ethereal layer in search of essence and treasure, among beasts more terrible than those they had left behind.

Each day, the Spires pulsed and the mist returned, swallowing the land they had reclaimed. None who ventured out escaped death. Their Lords endured, and the Realms grew stronger through what the expeditions recovered. Generations gave their lives for a future they would never see.

At last, the Realms raised Hyperstructures in the name of their Orders. Then came the day when the monuments were consumed and the Spires fell silent. The mist lifted, and this time it did not return. Balance had been restored.

Peace followed, but civilisation lay in ruins. The land had shifted once more. Beyond the Realms, the world waited to be rediscovered.

Age III

Eternum

Restoration & conquest

The return to greatness

The Realms came alive. With the Spires silent and the mist’s daily return ended, Lords set out to recover the world they had lost. They built settlements, opened roads and sought the greatness of the old kingdoms.

All the Hyperstructures had been destroyed. Yet their foundations remained, waiting to be uncovered. Lords began to rebuild them, hoping to rule as their predecessors had. When the first monument was completed, the Spires awoke. The portals opened once more.

Lords crossed the ethereal layer as freely as their own world. Society flourished. Tribes formed, trade grew and politics took hold. The old struggle for the Hyperstructures began again.

The Realms fought for resources, influence and domination. As the Lords approached victory, the world grew unstable once more.

The Lords had returned to their old ways. In their pursuit of power, they ignored the stories of calamities that had befallen the world generations before. The monuments glowed hot. The Spires rose from the ground. The world watched in awe.

When the victors had gathered their spoils, a flash of light swept across the land. A wave of heat followed. Then nothing. The world was gone. The Realms fell dormant.

Nothing but a single spark in the darkness.

Age IV

Dominion

The world that continues

The first adventurer

For millennia, the spark shone alone in the darkness.

Then, at last, someone stepped through. An adventurer.

And he was holding Loot.

From that single point, a new world begins. Adventurers explore outwards, reclaiming land and establishing society. Loot has returned, and the scattered peoples of the Lootverse have a world to build together.

The Realms will awaken here. This is the age of Dominion, beyond the cycles of conquest and collapse. Society takes root in a world that continues, its history carried forward by those who follow. What is built becomes something to inherit, defend and expand.

The story behind the story

The world we kept building

The four ages mirror the journey from Loot’s first burst of possibility to a world built through years of persistence. Their sequence follows that journey’s themes rather than the order in which the games were developed.

The ethereal layer gives the world’s underlying technology a visible presence. Its neon light and unfamiliar forms evoke the blockchain infrastructure beneath the medieval world. Contact brings danger, but also travel, wealth and discovery. Mastery of both worlds offers power; the pursuit of greatness carries a cost.

The beginning

Loot gives the world its foundation

Loot’s release in 2021 invited people to build games, characters and worlds around adventurer gear. Realms and Genesis Adventurers grew from that invitation. BibliothecaDAO pursued a connected world in which those creations could matter. [1] [2]

Blitz

The rush for power

The warring kingdoms reflect the speed and speculation of the NFT boom. Loot’s disappearance represents the fading of the momentum and shared promise that had drawn so many people together.

Frontier

Keeping the lights on

The mist reflects the bear market and the difficulty of turning that promise into lasting games. Funding supported experiments with uneven outcomes. Loot Survivor showed what could be made playable, while Realms contributors continued developing the world through repeated setbacks.

Eternum

Making the promise playable

Eternum brings that persistence into a playable world of production, trade, diplomacy and war. The flagship seasonal strategy game embodies the ambition to rebuild and bring the Realms to life. [3]

Dominion

Bringing Loot home

The spark carries the promise of a beginning beyond the seasonal cycles. Dominion carries the ambition of a persistent world shared by Loot, Realms, Genesis Adventurers, Crypts & Caverns, Beasts and other communities.

What carries between ages

What endures

Loot & the Orders

Loot’s origin is unknown. The sixteen Orders connect the ages even when history is lost. They give communities a shared identity across games. Their basis in Loot item suffixes connects them to the original collection. [1]

The Realms

The Realms endure while their rulers and surrounding geography change. They shelter civilisation through Frontier, but Eternum’s destruction leaves them dormant. They are absent from the long darkness that follows, awaiting their return in Dominion’s new world.

The Hyperstructures

The Hyperstructures embody power, balance and victory. Lords capture the ancient monuments in Blitz and rebuild from their foundations in Eternum. [4] Between these ages, Frontier’s survivors establish Hyperstructures for their Orders. Those monuments are consumed in the event that stills the Spires and ends the calamity.

The Spires & the mist

The Spires mark a collision between the known world and the ethereal layer. Hyperstructures bring their passages into balance, but the same portals admit things from beyond. The ethereal layer holds opportunity as well as danger. Frontier’s daily pulses renew a destructive mist, distinct from the ordinary mist at the edge of the unknown.

The gamesStory and gameplay

Blitz is the age of war, Frontier the age of endurance, Eternum the age of restoration, and Dominion the age of continuity. A Blitz match is one of the many struggles for ancient Hyperstructures. Seasons can be read as cycles through the first three ages; Dominion is intended to continue beyond them.

Blitz’s Season 0 Spires are dormant. In the Season 1 design, claiming a Hyperstructure activates them all, allowing travel between discovered Spires. Eternum begins with inactive Spires; completing its first Hyperstructure opens the portals. Its final calamity follows the contest and the claim period.

In Frontier, the player is the enduring Lord whose soldiers and expeditions perish. Resources strengthen the Realm; ethereal expeditions seek essence and chests of $LORDS. Establishing Hyperstructures increases an Order’s score. The Orders support collective endeavour in Frontier and Eternum, while Blitz remains a free-for-all.

The ethereal layer also connects the games to the wider ecosystem. Frontier’s beasts come from Loot Survivor and may return in Eternum. Eternum’s planned ethereal mines offer wrapped bitcoin. Other games, creatures, objects and currencies can enter through this same dimension.

Dominion’s base design begins ordinary adventurers without equipment; the Loot-bearing arrival marks the beginning of its age. Its expanding world and collection integrations remain in development.

Beyond the knownQuestions the world has not answered

Why did Loot vanish? What first brought the worlds together? Who raised the Spires, and what remains undiscovered beyond them? Did mastering the Hyperstructures cause the final calamity, or merely awaken it? Why did the Realms endure? Who is the first adventurer, and where did he find Loot?

The world’s history has left these questions unanswered.
