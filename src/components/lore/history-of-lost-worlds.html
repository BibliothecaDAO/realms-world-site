Before the ages

The constants

A world without a beginning

No one remembers how the world began. Its geography shifts, its landmarks vanish, and no traveller has found its edge. A person could walk through the mist forever without returning to where they started.

Through it all, Loot and the sixteen Orders have persisted. Some believe Loot is woven into the world itself: never truly lost, always found again. Others believe it created the world.

From Loot emerged the Realms. Its power established societies that endured through the ages. Kingdoms rose and fell, and the Realms changed hands, but they remained anchors in a shifting world. An ancient civilisation raised the Hyperstructures in devotion to Loot and the Orders. For millennia, these colossal monuments stood as centres of power and influence.

Then Loot vanished overnight. No one knew why. The societies it had sustained fractured, and the world clung to what remained: the Realms and the Hyperstructures.

Age I

Blitz

Chaos & war

The struggle for what remained

Thousands of kingdoms rose and fell in the struggle for the Hyperstructures. Their rulers sought power and answers. Perhaps the ancient monuments held a clue to Loot’s disappearance, or a path to its return.

Then the Spires emerged from the land. These otherworldly structures tore at the air and bent the laws of space and time. They opened passage through the ethereal plane, allowing travellers to move between distant Spires in an instant. Lords seized this advantage and turned it against their rivals.

As the Spires multiplied, the world warped further. The air grew toxic. A thick, dark mist spread across the land, choking the life from it until everything fell to darkness.

Everything except the Realms.

Age II

Frontier

The dark age

The last lights of the world

The last lights of the world shone within the Realms. Beyond them, the mist swallowed the ruins of civilisation, erasing its history with every place it destroyed.

Aspiring Lords ventured out to reestablish the frontier. They explored, gathered what they could and pushed back the darkness. But the mist returned again and again, killing those who ventured too far and swallowing what they had reclaimed.

Despite these losses, the Realms grew stronger. Eventually the mist lifted, and this time it did not return. The land had shifted once more, but the Spires had stabilised. The world lay open to exploration again.

Age III

Eternum

Restoration & conquest

The return to greatness

The Realms came alive. Lords set out to recover the world they had lost, uncovering the foundations of ruined Hyperstructures and restoring them to their former glory. With the Spires stable, they crossed the ethereal plane as freely as their own.

Society flourished. Tribes formed, trade grew and politics took hold. War followed. The Realms fought for resources, influence and domination until one tribe claimed ultimate victory.

The victors believed their mastery of the Hyperstructures would finally bring Loot home. The monuments glowed hot. The Spires rose from the ground. The world watched in awe.

A flash of light swept across the land. A wave of heat followed. Then nothing.

Nothing but a single spark in the darkness.

Age IV

Dominion

The world that continues

The first adventurer

The spark shone for an eternity, and then another.

Then, at last, someone stepped through. An adventurer.

And he was holding Loot.

From that single point, a new age begins. Adventurers explore outwards, reclaiming land and establishing society. Loot has returned, and the scattered peoples of the Lootverse have a world to build together.

This is the age of Dominion. Society takes root in a world that continues, its history carried forward by those who follow. What is built becomes something to inherit, defend and expand.

The story behind the story

The world we kept building

The four ages mirror the journey from Loot’s first burst of possibility to a world built through years of persistence. Their sequence follows that journey’s themes rather than the order in which the games were developed.

The beginning

Loot gives the world its foundation

Loot’s release in 2021 invited people to build games, characters and worlds around adventurer gear. Realms and Genesis Adventurers grew from that invitation. BibliothecaDAO pursued a connected world in which those creations could matter. [1] [2]

Blitz

The rush for power

The warring kingdoms reflect the speed and speculation of the NFT boom. Loot’s disappearance represents the fading of the momentum and shared promise that had drawn so many people together.

Frontier

Keeping the lights on

The mist reflects the bear market and the difficulty of turning that promise into lasting games. Funding supported experiments with uneven outcomes. Loot Survivor showed what could be made playable, while Realms contributors continued developing the world through repeated setbacks.

Eternum

Making the promise playable

Eternum brings that persistence into a playable world of production, trade, diplomacy and war. The flagship seasonal strategy game embodies the ambition to rebuild and bring the Realms to life. [3]

Dominion

Bringing Loot home

The spark turns victory into a beginning. Dominion, the working title for Eternal Game, carries the ambition of a persistent world shared by Loot, Realms, Genesis Adventurers, Crypts & Caverns, Beasts and other communities.

What carries between ages

What endures

Loot & the Orders

Loot’s origin is unknown. The sixteen Orders connect the ages even when history is lost. Their basis in Loot item suffixes gives this continuity a link to the original collection. [1]

The Realms

The Realms endure while their rulers and surrounding geography change. Their persistence gives civilisation somewhere to begin again, even when its people, buildings and institutions are lost.

The Hyperstructures

Ancient monuments become the focus of hope and conquest. The gameplay distinction supports the arc: Blitz starts with constructed Hyperstructures; Eternum asks players to complete them. [4]

The Spires & the mist

The Spires connect worlds and destabilise reality. The mist destroys land and memory. Ordinary mist at the edge of the unknown remains distinct from the toxic darkness of Frontier. Their precise origin and relationship remain unexplained.

Across the agesThe four Aspects

Four leading Aspects embody the forces that shape each age. They carry the Orders’ presence across time: the desire to restore, the drive to destroy, the skill to survive and the instinct to discover.

The gamesStory and gameplay

Blitz is the age of war, Frontier the age of endurance, Eternum the age of restoration, and Dominion the age of continuity. Their shared lore connects seasonal contests to the ambition of a persistent world.

The games express that history through different systems. Blitz’s Hyperstructures begin fully constructed; Eternum’s rise from discovered foundations. Spire travel appears in Eternum S2 design, and its introduction into Blitz remains planned. Dominion’s base design begins ordinary adventurers without equipment; the Loot-bearing arrival marks the beginning of its age.

Victory rules vary between versions of Eternum. The final calamity belongs to the world’s history. Dominion’s expanding world and collection integrations remain in development.

Beyond the knownQuestions the world has not answered

Why did Loot vanish? Who raised the Spires, and what lies beyond them? Did mastering the Hyperstructures cause the final calamity, or merely awaken it? Why did the Realms endure? Who is the first adventurer, and where did he find Loot?

The world’s history has left these questions unanswered.
